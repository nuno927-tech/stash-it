Meet Scout

A squirrel who knows exactly where he buried it.

Squirrels stash thousands of nuts a season and come back for them months later. Seemed like the right hire.

Scout keeps the ledger: what's covered, what's about to lapse, what's missing a receipt. When there's nothing left to do he falls asleep on the card — which is the most honest thing an app has ever told you.

What it does

Everything a claim asks for, in one place.

Not a filing cabinet. It knows what you own, what you pay for, and how long any of it is still good for.

A countdown, not a filing date

Every item shows what's left. Under six months it starts counting days, because "four months" and "three months" look identical at a glance.

One thing, five warranties

A couch with a lifetime frame, ten years on the cushions and one on the fabric. Scout tracks all five and warns you about whichever quits first.

Photograph it and move on

Product, serial plate, paperwork, several at once. Twenty seconds an item, which is the only reason anyone keeps doing it.

Receipts straight from your inbox

Share the order email to Stash it. Shop, date and price arrive filled in, attachment and all.

Organized by room

Because that's how you picture your house. "What's in the garage" is a scroll, not a search.

Backups you actually own

One file with everything in it. Back up now hands it to your share sheet — Drive, Files, an email to yourself. Wherever you keep things.

Passports, licenses, insurance

A passport tells you it expires in February. It doesn't tell you most countries want six months on it, or that renewal takes eight weeks. Stash it counts back from that and says when to start — and holds dates only, never a scan or a number.

What actually leaves your account

Not "$94 a month" — which month is the heavy one. Add your subscriptions and a six-month curve shows where three annual plans happen to land together, and what renews next.

Pricing

Free for most houses. $10 a year for full ones.

Twenty-five records free, forever — items, documents and subscriptions together. Past that, or if you'd rather Scout tapped you on the shoulder before something lapses, there's the paid tier.

Free — $0

Twenty-five records — items, documents and subscriptions, counted together. Every photo, receipt and manual you want to attach to them. Backups, offline, the fingerprint lock, the whole app. No account, no card, no expiry.

Pro — $10 a year

Or $1 a month, if you prefer. Unlimited records, for anyone whose garage alone runs past twenty-five. And notifications — told on the day something needs starting, with the app shut, rather than finding out next time you open it.

What subscribing changes about privacy

On the free tier nothing leaves your phone. Nothing. That doesn't change, and it never will.

Notifications are the exception, and they have to be — one cannot arrive from a phone that's switched off. So if you turn them on, exactly two things get sent: a delivery address your browser generates, and the days something is due. No email address, no name, no account. Not what the items are, not the photos, not what you paid.

What the notification says never leaves your phone. The wording is worked out on the device and kept in your browser's own cache; the message that arrives is empty and your phone fills it in. Nobody in the middle can read a notification they didn't write. Turn it off and the record is deleted.

That's the whole difference, and it's why notifications cost money — they're the only part of Stash it with a bill attached. The full policy spells out the three honest costs.

Questions

The things worth asking.

Where is my data actually stored?

In your browser's own storage, on the device you're holding. Installing it to your home screen tells the browser to treat that storage as permanent instead of something it can clear when space runs short.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Without a backup, it's gone. Being straight about that is part of the deal. Back up now, in Settings, makes one file and hands it to your share sheet — Drive, Files, email, wherever you keep things. Scout nags you when it's been a while.

Is my data encrypted?

No. It's stored the way every browser database is. The lock keeps the app shut, which stops someone who picks up your phone — not someone with your phone, a cable and a laptop. Claiming otherwise would be the kind of promise people plan around.

Do you sell or share anything?

There's nothing to sell. No analytics, no ad network, no data broker, and on the free tier no server to send anything to. If you turn notifications on, the app sends a delivery address and a list of dates — never your email, never the items themselves. That's the complete list, and the privacy policy is the long version.

Which devices does it work on?

Any modern browser — Android, iPhone, Windows, Mac, Linux. It installs to the home screen on Android, and on iPhone through Safari's Share → Add to Home Screen. Sharing receipts from email is Android-only, because iOS won't let web apps register as share targets.

Does my data sync between devices?

No, and that follows directly from there being no server. Each device keeps its own copy. Moving to a new phone is an export and a restore — about a minute.

What does setup involve?

Open the link, tell Scout your name, add something. No account, no verification, no configuration.

How do updates work?

Like a website. The new version arrives next time you open it, and your data is untouched.

What does it cost?

Nothing for twenty-five records — items, documents and subscriptions counted together — which covers most people. Pro is $10 a year or $1 a month, and adds unlimited records plus notifications when something needs starting. Stop paying and everything you've saved stays yours — editable, exportable, all of it. You just go back to being told inside the app instead of by a notification.
