Meet Scout

A squirrel who never forgets where he put things.

Squirrels cache thousands of nuts a season and remember where. It seemed like the right animal for an app about putting things somewhere safe and finding them again years later.

Scout is the one keeping watch. He shows you what's covered and what's lapsing, tells you which items are missing a receipt, and curls up and sleeps when there's genuinely nothing left for you to do — which is a state most apps never admit exists.

Privacy

No server. Nothing to leak.

Most apps in this category are a database of what's inside your house, sitting on somebody else's computer. This one isn't, because there isn't one.

It stays on your phone

Items, photos and documents are written to your device's own storage. Nothing is uploaded.

No account, ever

No email address, no password, no profile. There's nothing to sign up for and nothing to sign in to.

No analytics, no tracking

The app makes no network requests of its own. Nobody, including us, can tell what you own or that you opened it.

Your backup, your storage

Export one file and put it where you like. Connect Google Drive and it's your Drive, under your account.

Locked to your fingerprint

Turn on the biometric lock and the app won't open without your face or finger.

Yours to leave

Delete the app and the data goes with it. No export request, no support ticket, no waiting.

Being straight about the limits: the lock keeps the app shut, it doesn't encrypt the database — and because nothing is on a server, a lost phone with no backup means lost data. Export one. That's the trade you're making, and it should be yours to make.

Questions

The things worth asking.

Where is my data actually stored?

In your browser's own storage on the device you're using, in the same place a website keeps anything it saves locally. It isn't sent anywhere. Installing the app to your home screen tells the browser to treat that storage as permanent rather than something it may clear when space runs short.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Without a backup, the data is gone — and being straight about that is part of the trade. Export a copy from Settings and keep it somewhere, or connect your own Google Drive and send backups there. The app nudges you when it's been a while.

Is my data encrypted?

The database is not encrypted at rest; it's stored the way every browser database is. The biometric lock keeps the app shut, which stops someone who picks up your unlocked phone — it isn't a defence against someone with your phone, a cable and a laptop. Saying otherwise would be the kind of promise people plan around.

Do you sell or share anything?

There is nothing to sell. The app has no server, no account system and no analytics, so no data about you or your possessions reaches anyone. The only network requests it makes are ones you start — connecting Drive, or loading the page itself.

Which devices does it work on?

Any modern browser: Android, iPhone, Windows, Mac, Linux. It installs to the home screen on Android and on iPhone via Safari's Share → Add to Home Screen. Sharing receipts straight from email is Android-only for now, because iOS doesn't let web apps register as share targets.

Does my data sync between devices?

No — and that's the direct consequence of there being no server. Each device holds its own copy. Moving to a new phone means exporting a backup and restoring it, which takes about a minute.

What does setup involve?

Open the link, tell Scout your name, and add something. There's no account, no email verification and no configuration. Connecting Google Drive is optional and takes a few minutes of one-time setup.

How do updates work?

Like a website: the new version arrives the next time you open it, and it asks before reloading so it can't interrupt what you're doing. Your data is untouched by updates.

What does it cost?

Nothing. The free tier holds 15 items, and everything you've saved stays editable and exportable regardless. There's no server bill to pass on to anyone.
